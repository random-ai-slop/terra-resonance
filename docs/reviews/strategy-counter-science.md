# Product-sufficiency counter-review: scientific value and hidden complexity

Historical planning review of STRATEGY, ROADMAP, strategy-decisions and the three roadmap studies, 2026-09-04. This was not a repeat of the previous numerical bug lists; existing code investment did not automatically justify a feature.

**Conclusion: accept strengthening the first delivery, but reusing evaluate_field does not make every addition small or scientifically safe.** Keep nodes clearly named, trajectories consistent with displayed displacement/time, and six lessons falsifiable. Reject implicit support for every new overlay in every format. Interoperability/source work can partly overlap; weak-Q maturation need not await all kernels/observations. After the changes below, implementation evidence should replace further generic strategy documents.

## Scientific claims behind the six lessons

1. Breathing: 0R0 with explicit deformation amplification. Quarter-period zero displacement usually has nonzero velocity; do not say motion stops without a velocity observation.
2. T: linear displacement has zero radial component. Finite display gain in x+δu_t causes second-order radius change, so a perfectly unchanged silhouette is not required. Do not silently project back to the sphere to enforce one.
3. n/l/m: different m in one SNREI n/l share frequency as a degenerate angular basis, not rotation splitting. Radial node counts are observations, not universal definitions of n; specify held-fixed variables.
4. Liquid-core sides: use explicit layer IDs/sides, not an outer-snapped point pretending to compare both. R/S normal continuity, possible tangential jumps and zero fluid T support are separate statements.
5. Standing/traveling: same mode ±m, equal frequency, quadrature phase and q=null for the closed example. Pattern motion differs from material trajectory; nearby different n/l frequencies are not one degenerate traveling subspace.
6. Beats: different eigenfunctions generally have no universal global envelope. Bind the claim to one material point/component where both terms are nonzero; disabling either term removes the local beat. Distinguish monotonic Q decay, using q=null for the first case.

These require correct explanations, not new advanced physics. One counterexample per lesson has more value than ten extra controls.

## Is the first delivery sufficient?

With point–curve–trajectory links, node explanation and a perturbation recipe, it adequately serves the user's reference-like but more realistic/configurable/research/production goal. Do not add real source synthesis, mature density/interface kernels, dual viewports or 3D coupling to the initial mandatory scope: current task completion does not depend on those new scientific objects.

Do not reduce it to a few selected demonstrations either. Complete configurable Model/SolveSpec, real internal fields, exportable scientific arrays, quality evidence and independent installed use are fundamental research commitments, above every optional nodal-art variation. If effort tightens, narrow general node algorithms and unnecessary overlay/format combinations before cutting solver topology, model parameters or required media formats.

## Route ordering

Accept interoperability and material sensitivity immediately after a measurable baseline: they distinguish algorithm/model differences and parameter/mode responses with independent references/finite differences. Performance improvements should follow measured waits without indefinitely delaying science.

Change any serial B→C interpretation: source excitation needs canonical eigenfunctions, spatial derivatives, source/receiver coordinates and an independent MINEOS synthesis case; it does not need every density/interface kernel or a general sensitivity framework. A reusable B1 source/station path is enough to begin. Advanced observation operators follow simple three-component synthesis.

Weak-Q frequency/attenuation needs its bulk/shear dissipative weights and limits, not mature density/interface kernels. Let those quantities advance first; keep unverified experimental Q labeled accordingly rather than claiming inversion sensitivity from a frequency-shift feature.

Accept radial anisotropy before complex rheology/3D when useful: clear Earth-model value, bounded radial data extension and independent MINEOS comparison. Do not permanently postpone it for visually dramatic rotation or prematurely add arbitrary fourth-order tensor fields to the baseline.

Complex rheology and slow rotation are parallel research choices, not an obligatory queue. Their complex-data and validation gates remain distinct; this does not promote them into the first delivery. Direct 3D and inversion require specific questions, models/data and independent evidence. Results can be imported/exported without claiming the package validates their solver. Preserve CoupledMode/MeshMode boundaries instead of universal metadata.

## Two expansions to reject

Reject “nodes and trajectories are nearly free, so every output must reproduce everything live.” Degenerate zeros, discontinuities, deformation maps and animated GLB lines have numerical/format costs. Freeze the smallest useful representation and state capabilities rather than inventing separate algorithms in every renderer.

Reject “material perturbation automatically explains mechanism.” Δf/f reports a response under one parameterization, not uniqueness, validated kernels or branch tracking. Preserve request, step, pair and numerical error without generating unsupported causal explanations.

## Conditions for implementation

Merge component/coordinate node scope, trajectory gain/window, lesson preconditions, correct perturbation checks, output capabilities and nonserial source/kernel/rheology dependencies into one concise scope and acceptance plan. No generic object framework is necessary. Then use real PREM and a question-led project to test the full design. At that point the biggest uncertainties were numerical execution and product integration, not the absence of another roadmap.
